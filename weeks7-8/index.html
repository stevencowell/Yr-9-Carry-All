Weeks 7-8: Accurate marking and hand-tool control

Establish reliable references, preserve the line and refine the required rebates and housings through controlled checks.

Theory 1

Establishing reference faces and marking accurately

Accurate Carry-All components begin with consistent reference surfaces. The face side is the selected flat surface used as the main reference, while the face edge is a straight edge confirmed as square to it. These surfaces should be clearly marked and used whenever measurements or lines are transferred. Working from the same references helps the sides, handle panel, dividers, joints, rebates and housings align correctly during dry assembly.

A try square is used to mark or check lines at a right angle to the face edge and to check whether adjoining surfaces are square. The stock and tool must be clean, stable and held correctly so the square sits firmly against the reference edge. A marking gauge can produce a line parallel to the face side or face edge, such as when setting out matching features. Its setting should be taken from verified drawing information or teacher direction and tested before marking important project material.

Different lines serve different purposes. A sharp pencil provides a clear, visible line that can be corrected when necessary. A marking knife can create a finer line and define an exact boundary, but it must be used only through the teacher-approved process. Whichever method is selected, the line has width. Decide which edge of the line represents the required size and mark the waste side clearly. Removing material from the wrong side can make a component undersized or cause a joint to fit poorly.

  • Select and mark the face side and face edge before setting out features.
  • Measure and mark from the same reference surfaces wherever possible.
  • Use the try square to carry lines accurately across required faces.
  • Mark waste areas clearly before any material is removed.
  • Cross-check matching Carry-All components before practical work continues.

Repeated dimensions need a consistent method. Where several features must match, use one verified gauge setting, template or teacher-approved reference rather than measuring each position separately. Measuring from one newly marked line to the next can create cumulative error: several small inaccuracies combine into a larger mismatch by the final feature. Where the approved layout allows, take positions from one common datum so each measurement remains independent of earlier marking errors.

Marking should be inspected before material is removed. Check component identity, orientation, reference marks, written dimensions, joint arrangement and waste areas against the working drawing and teacher directions. Do not scale the drawing or invent a missing measurement. Useful folio evidence can show the reference symbols, marked waste and a caption explaining how cumulative error was avoided. Careful marking saves material, reduces correction work and makes accurate assembly far more likely.

Key idea: Establish one reliable face side and face edge, then make every measurement, square line and repeated feature from consistent verified references.

Theory 2

Controlling approved cuts for rebate-and-housing features

A tenon saw can be used for controlled, accurate cuts when forming approved Carry-All joint features. Accuracy begins before the saw touches the timber. The component must be correctly identified, its reference faces and waste areas clearly marked, and the layout checked against the working drawing and teacher directions. The workpiece must then be held securely using the teacher-approved method so it cannot shift, vibrate or tip during the cut.

A balanced stance helps the saw travel in a straight and controlled path. Position yourself so you can see the marked line clearly without leaning awkwardly or placing any part of your body in the cutting path. Your arm, wrist and saw should move naturally in line with the intended cut. Local bench arrangements and workholding procedures may differ, so follow the school SOP, teacher demonstration and SWMS rather than copying an unapproved technique.

The cut should begin on the approved waste side of the line. The layout line represents the boundary of the required component or joint surface and should be preserved for checking. Start with controlled movement so the saw establishes a reliable path before the cut deepens. Once started, follow the approved waste-side position while checking the saw against the visible lines on the relevant faces. Do not deliberately cut through the line simply to finish more quickly.

  • Confirm the component, orientation, reference marks and waste area.
  • Secure the timber using the teacher-approved workholding method.
  • Adopt a stable stance with a clear view of the layout line.
  • Begin carefully on the approved waste side and preserve the line.
  • Pause to check direction, depth and alignment rather than forcing the saw.

Forcing the saw usually reduces control. Excessive pressure can make the blade wander, damage the timber fibres or carry the cut beyond its intended boundary. A saw that binds, jumps or moves away from the correct path is a reason to stop and inspect the setup. Check the workholding, stance, saw path and markings, then seek teacher guidance before continuing. Trying to twist the blade back towards the line while cutting can make the error worse.

After the cut, inspect both visible faces and compare the result with the preserved layout lines. Look for wandering, an angled cut, damage at the edge or material removed from the required side. Joint accuracy depends on controlled cuts that leave enough material for the approved checking and fitting process. A useful folio record should show the marked waste side and explain how the cut was checked before further material was removed.

Key idea: Secure the work, use a balanced stance and follow the approved waste side while preserving the layout line for checking.

Theory 3

Refining required rebates and housings

Chiselling may be used through the teacher-approved process to refine required Carry-All rebate and housing surfaces after the main waste has been removed. The aim is not to reshape the feature quickly, but to bring uneven or remaining material carefully towards the verified layout line. This process is often called paring to the line. The line should remain visible until the final checking stage because it shows the intended boundary and helps prevent too much material from being removed.

The workpiece must be properly supported and secured using the teacher-approved method. Unsupported timber can move, split or break near a rebate or housing, particularly around narrow shoulders, recesses or edges. Your hands and body must remain outside the cutting path, and the exact chisel position, direction and handling technique must follow the teacher demonstration, school SOP and SWMS. A sharp tool should only be used through the approved local process.

Grain direction affects how timber responds to a chisel. Cutting carelessly with the grain may cause fibres to split beyond the intended line, while working against difficult grain can make the tool dig in or tear the surface. Before removing material, inspect the grain on the relevant face and consider where the fibres may run. Approach fragile edges cautiously and stop if the grain begins lifting, splitting or behaving differently from what was expected.

  • Identify the high spot or remaining waste before touching the feature.
  • Secure and support the component using the approved method.
  • Remove only a small amount of material at a time.
  • Preserve the layout line and check the shoulder or depth surface frequently.
  • Test the rebate or housing fit gently during dry assembly and never force it.

Fine-tuning should follow a repeated cycle: inspect, remove a small amount, clear the surface and check again. Removing a large amount because a fit feels tight can quickly turn a correctable result into a loose one. An oversized rebate or housing cannot be restored by removing more timber. Adhesive is not a substitute for accurate fitting, and excessive gaps may weaken alignment or reduce the quality of the finished corner.

During dry fitting, look for the exact point where the rebate or housing binds rather than assuming the whole surface is too tight. Check component orientation, transferred lines, contact marks and whether the parts are entering squarely. If the cause is unclear, stop and seek teacher guidance before making another adjustment. Useful process evidence should explain what was checked, what small correction was approved and how the fit improved without damaging the required feature.

Key idea: Refine required rebates and housings by supporting the work, reading the grain and removing only small amounts while preserving the line and checking the fit repeatedly.
